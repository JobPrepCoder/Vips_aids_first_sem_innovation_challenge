The challenge

Invisible AI. Visible impact.

AI doesn't need to look like a robot or a chatbot. It can quietly help someone find a safer route, reduce waste, understand a confusing process, or make a better decision.

Think beyond the obvious

Start with people

Who experiences the problem? When does it happen? What do they do today, and why isn't that enough?

Keep it grounded

Build a plausible solution

You can propose an app, sensor, simple website, data-driven tool, or even a low-cost non-AI system. Explain where AI genuinely helps.

Make your thinking visible

Explain your choices

We care about how you understand the problem, why your solution could work, and how you would test it—not just fancy technology.

Starter inspiration

Three everyday problems worth solving.

These are examples to spark your thinking, not ready-made projects to copy. Pick a problem you have observed yourself and make the idea your own.

Example 01 · Mobility

Where is the safest walking route?

Problem: Students walking to campus may face broken footpaths, dark stretches, or unsafe crossings—but don't know which route is safer.

Possible solution

A community-powered route helper that combines reported hazards, lighting, footpath quality and time of day to flag safer options. Start with a map and sample data.

HTML/CSS/JSMaps APIOptional ML ranking
Example 02 · Sustainability

Can we reduce canteen food waste?

Problem: Food may be prepared in quantities that don't match demand, while students and canteen staff have little visibility into likely leftovers.

Possible solution

A simple dashboard where daily meal counts are recorded and a basic forecast estimates demand. Staff can compare predictions with actual servings and adjust tomorrow's plan.

PythonCSV / SheetsSimple forecasting
Example 03 · Student life

How do students find the right campus information?

Problem: New students may miss deadlines or struggle to find the right form, office, lab rule, or event information across different notices.

Possible solution

A searchable campus guide that answers questions only from verified notices and links to the original source. If information is missing, it should say so—not guess.

JavaScriptSearch / FAQOptional RAG
Remember: AI is optional if it doesn't add value. A clear, useful solution with a good explanation is stronger than adding AI just to use the label.
From observation to submission

Four steps. One thoughtful idea.

You are not expected to be an expert in your first semester. Be curious, be honest about what you know, and explain your reasoning clearly.

01

Find a problem

Observe your campus, home, neighbourhood or daily routine. Describe who is affected and why it matters.

02

Design a solution

Sketch the workflow. Explain what the user does, what the system does, and how the result helps.

03

Choose your tech

Name the languages, tools, APIs, datasets or AI methods you would use—and why each is appropriate.

04

Record your pitch

Upload your code to GitHub and record a 3–5 minute video explaining the problem and your solution.

Submission deadline

Bring us your point of view.

Submit both required links by 25 October. Your project can be a prototype, proof of concept, or a well-explained design—make your work and reasoning clear.

What this activity is really about

We evaluate your thinking—not just your code.

The goal is to discover how you observe the world, reason about a problem, and turn an idea into a plausible solution.

40%

Problem understanding

Is the problem real and specific? Have you identified the people affected, the context, and why current approaches fall short?

35%

Solution thinking

Is the idea useful, feasible and well reasoned? Does it address the root problem, and can you explain how you would test it?

25%

Communication & technical choices

Can you explain the workflow, GitHub code and technology stack clearly, including limitations and responsible use?

The central question: “How well can you identify a meaningful problem and think through a solution?” A small, thoughtful idea can have more value than a complicated project without a clear purpose.
